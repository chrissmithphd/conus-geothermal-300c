## Technology Basis

### Power Output Per Well

**Conventional Hydrothermal (150-200°C):**
- 4-10 MW per well
- Example: The Geysers, CA averages 4 MW/well

**Superhot Geothermal (300-400°C):**
- 15-30 MW per well (estimated)
- Limited operational data; scaling from conventional

**Supercritical (>374°C):**
- 36-45 MW per well (demonstrated)
- IDDP-2, Iceland: 45 MW from single well at 427°C
- **5-10× output vs conventional wells**

### Well Spacing & Development

**Conventional Systems:**
- The Geysers: 3.1 wells/km² (0.32 km²/well)
- Power density: ~12.5 MW/km²

**Superhot Systems (300-400°C):**
- Estimated: 1-2 wells/km² (0.5-1.0 km²/well)
- Power density: **30-40 MW/km²**
- Higher output per well → fewer wells needed

**Supercritical Systems (>374°C):**
- Estimated: 0.5-1 well/km² (1.0-2.0 km²/well)  
- Power density: 25-35 MW/km²
- Very high output per well but technically challenging

## Key Uncertainties

### Technical

1. **Drilling Costs:** Currently $10-20M per well at 7-10 km depth
   - Need 50% cost reduction for economic viability
   - Oil & gas technology transfer critical

2. **EGS Success Rate:** Enhanced Geothermal Systems require fracturing
   - Current success rate: 60-80%
   - Need to improve reservoir connectivity

3. **Supercritical Wells:** Only 2 demonstrated (IDDP-1, IDDP-2)
   - Corrosion challenges at >374°C
   - Long-term performance unknown

### Economic

1. **Competition with Renewables:** Solar/wind costs declining faster than projected
   - Geothermal advantage: Dispatchable baseload
   - Value increases in high-renewable grids

2. **Transmission:** Western resources far from eastern demand
   - Need major HVDC transmission build-out
   - Or develop hydrogen production in West for transport

3. **Development Timeline:** 10-15 years per large project
   - Exploration, permitting, drilling, construction
   - Slower than solar/wind (2-5 years)

### Policy

1. **Federal Land Access:** Much geothermal potential on BLM land
   - Permitting streamlining needed
   - Environmental review timelines

2. **Tax Credits:** Current ITC/PTC support
   - Need parity with solar/wind
   - Bonus for superhot/supercritical development

3. **R&D Funding:** DOE Geothermal Technologies Office
   - Current: ~$100M/year
   - Recommended: $500M-1B/year for 10 years

## Bottom Line

**The accessible superhot geothermal resource in the western US could supply 3,000 GW with proven drilling (≤7 km) and 10,000+ GW with frontier drilling (≤10 km).** 

That's enough to:
- ✅ Replace all US coal and gas generation (696 GW)
- ✅ Electrify transportation (adding ~500 GW demand)
- ✅ Power industrial decarbonization (hydrogen, steel, cement)
- ✅ Export clean electricity or hydrogen to global markets

The limitation is not the resource—it's the **drilling technology, cost, and pace of deployment**.
